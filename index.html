Geist

Geist is a research system for people who cover public companies. It keeps the facts, evidence, and your view of a company connected as new information arrives.

Try Geist (Work in Progress)

Research

Ask what a company reported and Geist returns the figure, the fiscal period, and the exact place it came from in the SEC filing.

The source is part of the answer. You can open the filing and inspect the evidence directly. If Geist cannot establish the fact from the available evidence, it refuses rather than filling in the gap.

Memo

Research does not end when a question is answered.

You can keep the evidence in a living memo for the company: what you believe, what supports or challenges that view, what could change your mind, and what you want to watch.

Geist does not produce a rating or make the investment decision. The memo stays with the analyst.

New filings

When the company reports again, you can bring the new filing back to the research you already have instead of starting from another summary.

Geist can surface evidence that supports the existing view, contradicts it, or leaves the question unresolved. Each filing becomes another piece of evidence in the same body of research.

Why the evidence matters

I asked Perplexity and Geist the same question about Microsoft's reported cloud revenue. They returned different answers. I assumed my system was wrong, checked the filing manually, and found that Geist had resolved the right figure.

The same question asked to Perplexity

This is because a real number from a real filing can still be the wrong answer. It can belong to a different metric, fiscal period, segment, or column in a table. A citation alone does not resolve those relationships.

Models help interpret questions, search, and communicate results, but they are not the source of truth. Geist establishes financial facts against the underlying filing and structured SEC data before returning them.

Ryse, Inc.

I joined Ryse after meeting founder Mario Pardo following a quantitative finance lecture at Fordham. The first problem he gave me was a dependence model for understanding how assets behave together during stressed markets.

The original implementation operated on a small number of assets. I rebuilt the work in Python and wrote the infrastructure for fitting multiple copula families and estimating their parameters by maximum likelihood. I then generalized the system to run pairwise analysis and Monte Carlo simulations across the S&P 500.

The work let us compare dependence structures across different market regimes, including the dot-com crash, the 2008 financial crisis, COVID, and the 2025 tariff period. Under stressed conditions, the results showed why Gaussian dependence assumptions could materially understate joint tail behavior.

The implementation was incorporated into Ryse's proprietary risk software. Mario later presented the research at the Bloomberg Quant Seminar Series.

Bloomberg Quant Seminar Series | Ryse CEO Mario Pardo presenting research I conducted at Ryse on nonlinear risk and tail dependence across the S&P 500
